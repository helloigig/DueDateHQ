# Pitch — 1 Minute Elevator: DueDateHQ

*Skill: startup-pitch | Generated: 2026-05-05 (v2 — internal narrative, no ask)*

Two versions — formal and casual. ~150 words each, ~60 seconds at 150 wpm. Internal rehearsal — for when someone asks "what are you building?" not for cold investors.

---

## Version A — Formal (peer / partner / advisor)

> DueDateHQ is the client intelligence layer for solo and small-firm CPAs. The bottleneck for a CPA isn't quality — it's supply-side capacity. She runs fine-grained client service for 10 clients; she physically can't replicate that across 100.
>
> So we run the parts of that service a machine can do — multi-year client memory, per-client timing predictions, 100% materials confirmation, real-time 50-state rule monitoring, and drafted client emails — across her entire book. Last week, Louisiana announced a hurricane extension; our user saw her six affected clients and six pre-drafted emails within four hours. Her peers using Karbon found out from a client complaint.
>
> The whole thing is grounded in a 660-line user-research synthesis with a 600-client design partner. **The product is the customer's words made into software.**
>
> Dashboard shipped. **Want to see it?**

---

## Version B — Casual (hallway, coffee)

> You know how every CPA tool out there assumes the CPA *picks* which clients get the careful work? They don't. They just run out of hours. So all the AI-for-CPA companies are solving the wrong problem.
>
> We took the parts of "good client service" that a machine can do — remembering history, predicting when stuff is due, checking what's missing, watching state rule changes, drafting the chase emails — and we run them across the whole book. Every client gets the attention today only the top 10 get.
>
> Our design partner — 15-year CPA, 600 clients — said *"solve this one pain and I'm your first customer."* So we built the architecture around that exact pain.
>
> **Want to see it?**

## Delivery notes

**Both versions:**
- End with a question. Always.
- Pause after "wrong problem" / "across the whole book" — that's the insight. Let it land.
- Quote the customer once. *"Solve this one pain..."* in casual; *"the customer's words made into software"* in formal.

**Formal:**
- Slower pace, ~140 wpm.
- Strongest closer: *"Want to see it?"* — converting from talker to demo-ready signals confidence.

**Casual:**
- Conversational pace, ~160 wpm.
- The "you know how" opener invites them in. The Yan Jing quote provides authenticity in 12 words.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- ❌ "We use AI to..." — every pitch says this; it's invisible.
- ❌ "We're like X for Y" — only works if they instinctively get X. CPA market is too niche.
- ❌ Naming five competitors. One is enough.
- ❌ Quoting more than one customer line. One is signal; three is rambling.
