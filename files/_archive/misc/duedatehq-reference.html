01 — Thesis

One-sentence product thesis.

The North-Star selling point isn't technology. It's the outcome a CPA gets when your product works.

The promise

You will never be the last CPA in your state to find out about a filing extension — and when an announcement drops, you'll see the list of your affected clients within 24 hours, not by reading news.

The weaker framings to avoid

Avoid: "A lightweight, cloud-based tax deadline tracker." (Describes File In Time equally well.)
Avoid: "AI-powered tax compliance using Gemini/Grok." (Sells the tech, not the outcome.)
Avoid: "Never miss a tax deadline." (Table-stakes, no defensible position.)

Why this framing wins

Every competitor can claim "don't miss deadlines" — that's what a calendar does. What no competitor does today is continuously watch 50 state tax agencies for announcements and cross-reference them against your client portfolio inside 24 hours. That capability is only affordable because LLMs can read government announcement language and structurally identify impact. Without AI, this costs a human compliance team; with AI, it costs $49/month. The thesis and the economics are the same argument.

05 — User Stories

Three stories, three loops.

Daily use (triage), one-time use (onboarding), rare-but-defining use (state authority response). Each loop is a different product surface and a different engineering challenge.

Story 01 · Daily loop

Weekly triage during filing season

P0 Core
As a solo CPA with 80 clients,
I want to see all deadlines needing action this week within 30 seconds of opening my laptop,
so that I can decide my priorities without switching between Excel, Outlook, and handwritten notes.

Acceptance Criteria

  • Default dashboard groups deadlines into "Due this week / This month / Long term"
  • This-week items show day-level countdown
  • Filter by client, state, form type — response time < 1 second
  • Every deadline supports one-click: Completed · Deferred · In-progress
  • Full weekly triage takes < 5 minutes (vs. 45 today)
Main dashboard Three-tier grouping Status management Filter Smart priority ranking
Story 02 · One-time loop

Import 30 clients from a competitor in 30 minutes

P0 Core
As a CPA just switching from TaxDome,
I want to import 30 clients in 30 minutes and auto-generate their full-year deadline calendar,
so that I can start using the product immediately, not spend a week on data entry.

Acceptance Criteria

  • Supports CSV exports from TaxDome / Drake / Karbon / QuickBooks
  • Auto-detects field mappings (name, EIN, state, entity type)
  • For fuzzy or missing fields, offers smart suggestions — never blocks with errors
  • Post-import: full-year deadline calendar is ready immediately, no extra config
  • P95 completion ≤ 30 minutes (30-client benchmark)
CSV import Field mapping Calendar auto-generation Entity-type auto-recognition Field smart matching
Story 03 · Rare-but-defining loop

24-hour response to state tax authority announcement

P1 Differentiator
As a CPA with multi-state clients,
I want to receive a list of all affected clients within 24 hours of any state extension or policy announcement,
so that I can notify clients immediately without browsing 50 state tax websites daily.

Acceptance Criteria

  • System captures state tax authority official announcements within 24h
  • Auto-determines which clients are affected (state + county + entity + tax type)
  • Top-of-dashboard banner + email notification
  • "View affected clients" + "Batch-adjust deadlines" one-click actions
  • Every announcement links to the official source for manual verification
Live bulletin banner Affected-client matching Official source link Announcement auto-monitoring Announcement semantic parsing Impact-scope identification
The framing question worth raising in your presentation

The brief marks Story 1 as P0/Core and Story 3 as P1/Differentiator. But Story 3 is the business — the thing no competitor has. A tier-1 move is to argue: Story 1 is the shell, Story 3 is the spine.

07 — AI Leverage Points

Where LLMs are the business model, not the bow on top.

Nine ✦ items in the VPC. All solve one kind of problem: turning dispersed, obscure, high-dimensional government data into actionable intel for one specific CPA's client list.

Without AI, DueDateHQ doesn't exist at $49/month.
With AI, it has a moat.

Maintaining an accurate, real-time 50-state tax deadline database used to require a dedicated compliance team. LLM + automated monitoring reduces this by ~80%, which is what lets the unit economics work at solo-CPA pricing. The AI argument is the business-model argument.

The nine ✦ items

  1. Announcement auto-monitoring across 50+ state tax agency websites.
  2. Announcement semantic parsing — reading government language and extracting structured meaning.
  3. Impact-scope identification — translating "disaster relief for X county" into "here are your 6 clients affected."
  4. Affected-client matching — state × county × entity × tax type joins.

  7. Smart priority ranking — penalty size × client importance × historical delay patterns.
  8. Natural-language query assistant — "Which clients need to file PTE?"
  9. LLM-driven cross-referencing between announcements and client portfolios.

10 — Why Now

Three structural forces, simultaneously mature.

This isn't a bet on a new category — it's a modern replacement for a validated need where the incumbent is a desktop installer.

1. AI lowered the data-maintenance cost.

Maintaining an accurate, real-time 50-state deadline database used to need a dedicated compliance team. LLMs plus automated monitoring reduce that cost by roughly 80% — which is exactly what makes the unit economics work at solo-CPA pricing. Five years ago, this business couldn't exist at $49/month.

2. Small-business growth keeps compounding.

The US has added 5M+ new business applications per year since 2020. Each new small business creates downstream work for a CPA, and an increasing share operate in multiple states from day one. Demand is structurally rising.

3. Vertical SaaS pricing logic is validated.

Toast, SimplePractice, Jobber all proved that deep solutions to narrow industry pains generate extraordinary retention. Tax compliance is the least-negotiable part of a CPA's work — a natural stickiness anchor.

The framing sentence from the business plan worth memorising

"The most direct competitor, File In Time, is a desktop installer still being sold in 2026. That's not a threat — it's an invitation."

Who exactly to search for.

Green flags (send to these)

  • Title includes "Owner," "Founder," "Principal," or "Partner" + "CPA" or "EA"
  • Firm name is [LastName] CPA, PLLC · [LastName] & Associates · [City] Tax & Accounting
  • Headline mentions "small business," "multi-state," "PTE," "S-corp," or "entrepreneurs"
  • Posts recently about tax law changes, extensions, or state-specific issues
  • Firm website linked and looks active
  • Based in a state with heavy multi-state activity (TX, CA, FL, NY, IL)
  • Posted in the past 3 months (signals they're actively vocal)

Red flags (skip these)

  • Big 4 employees — Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG (wrong workflow, not buyers)
  • Mid-tier national firms — RSM, BDO, Grant Thornton, Baker Tilly, CLA
  • "Staff Accountant" or "Senior Accountant" at a large firm (not the decision-maker)
  • Corporate in-house accountants ("Accountant at Microsoft")
  • CPA exam candidates or accounting students
  • Pure bookkeepers with no tax work mentioned
  • Profile last active more than 6 months ago

The golden signal

CPAs who publicly post content about their work on LinkedIn or Twitter/X. They're already vocal, which selects for people willing to talk to strangers. Search LinkedIn's content feed for hashtags like #tax #CPA #smallbusiness, pick recent posters, and DM them referring to a specific thing they wrote. Response rates here are 3–5× higher than cold.

Faster alternative: the Taxpros Discord

The Taxpros Discord (taxprosdiscord.com) is all verified tax pros. Real-time conversation. Often faster than Reddit or LinkedIn because the friction is lower. Request an invite through the form, lurk 60 minutes to learn the register, then ask. Expect replies within hours.

16 — Interview Prep

Before the call.

The quality difference between a wasted 20 minutes and a gold-mine 20 minutes is entirely in what you ask. Specific moments, not abstract pain.

Rules that matter more than questions

Never ask "what are your pain points?" — you'll get abstract, useless answers. Instead ask about specific recent moments. "Walk me through last Monday morning." "Tell me about the last time something went wrong."

Shut up for 60% of the call. Silence after a question pulls out the real answer. Don't fill gaps.

Don't describe the product. If they ask what you're building, keep it to one sentence and redirect. You're there to learn, not to pitch. If they ask to see the product, say "I'm earlier than that — I'm talking with people first."

Record or take dense notes. You'll forget specifics within 24 hours. Verbatim quotes are gold for your presentation.

Warm-up · The last real moment
  • Walk me through last Monday morning — from when you opened your laptop until lunch.
  • How many clients are you currently serving? Roughly how many states?
  • What did the last 2 weeks of filing season look like for you?
Core · Deadline pain
  • Tell me about the last time you almost missed a deadline. What actually went wrong?
  • When was the last time a client came to you and said "didn't you know about X?" — and you hadn't?
  • How do you currently find out when a state tax authority announces an extension or policy change?
  • Think of a recent state announcement — walk me through what you did when you heard about it.
Tooling reality
  • What does your current setup look like? Can you share screen and show me?
  • If this tool vanished tomorrow, what would break first?
  • Have you tried [File In Time / Karbon / Canopy]? What made you stop or not switch?
  • What's the most valuable thing your current tool does? What does it not do that it should?
Edges and anomalies
  • Which clients give you the most anxiety? Why?
  • If you had to hand off your practice for a month, what would the handoff doc need to cover?
  • What's the weirdest state-specific filing requirement you've run into recently?
  • What do you wish software built for accountants understood, that it never seems to?
Closing
  • Who else should I be talking to? Anyone come to mind?
  • Would you be open to a quick follow-up in 2–3 weeks to see what I build?

You don't need to understand these deeply — you need to not waste your interviewee's time asking what each acronym means. 20 minutes of Googling each is sufficient.

Forms they file

1040 Individual income tax return. Apr 15 deadline. The thing every human in the US files.
1065 Partnership return. Mar 15 deadline.
1120-S S-corporation return. Mar 15 deadline.
1120 C-corporation return. Apr 15 deadline.
941 Quarterly payroll tax return. Due end of month after quarter.
1099 Miscellaneous income reporting. Jan 31 deadline.
K-1 Partner / shareholder income report. Flows from 1065 / 1120-S to personal 1040.
W-2 Employee wage statement. Jan 31 deadline.

Concepts you'll hear

PTE Pass-Through Entity election. State-level tax workaround for SALT cap. Election deadlines vary by state — a huge pain and a key feature target.
SALT State And Local Tax. Federal cap limits deduction to $10K. PTE is the workaround.
Nexus When a business has enough presence in a state to owe tax there. Determines which states a client must file in.
Franchise tax State-level tax for operating as an entity. Texas, California, Delaware all have different rules.
Estimated tax Quarterly prepayment for self-employed and pass-through owners. Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, Jan 15.
Extension 6-month filing delay, not a payment delay. Sep 15 (1065/1120-S) / Oct 15 (1040).
Disaster extension IRS grants automatic extension for federally declared disaster areas. This is where Story 3 lives.
EA Enrolled Agent. Federally licensed tax-only practitioner. Adjacent target user.

Timing they live by

Busy season Jan–Apr 15. 20+ hrs overtime weekly. Everyone is exhausted.
Extension crunch Sep–Oct 15. Second wave of deadline stress.
Shoulder season May–Aug & Nov–Dec. Best window for research, sales, and education.
Right now · late April 2026 Just past Apr 15. Exhausted but actually more available than during season.
