# Pricing Landscape: Tax Deadline Tracking Software
*Skill: startup-competitors | Generated: 2026-04-23*

## Market Pricing Overview

- **Dominant value metric:** Per-seat monthly subscription (3 of 4 competitors) **[Data]**. File In Time is the outlier with a perpetual license + annual maintenance model **[Data — timevalue.com]**.
- **Price range:** $8/user/mo (File In Time amortized Year 2+) to $149/user/mo (Canopy Premium) **[Data + Estimate]**
- **Median price point (standard tier):** $74/user/mo **[Data]** — driven by Canopy Standard, TaxDome Pro ($83), and Karbon Business ($89) clustering.
- **Free tier prevalence:** 0 of 4 competitors offer a true free tier **[Data — vendor pricing pages]**. Canopy offers 5 free Smart Intake credits on every plan; that's consumption-based, not a free tier.

**Key tier notes:**

- **File In Time amortization:** Year 1 all-in = ~$25/user/mo equivalent ($199 + $100 maintenance / 12). Years 2+ = ~$8/user/mo (maintenance only). Per-seat pricing is linear with no volume discount through 10 seats. **[Data + Estimate]**
- **Karbon billing flexibility:** Monthly billing costs ~34% more than annual. Business tier is marketed as "most popular." **[Data — karbonhq.com/pricing]**
- **TaxDome structural constraint:** No monthly option at any tier. 3-year commits unlock ~12.5% additional savings. Seasonal seat option ($85/mo Pro or $500 for 4-month Business term) exists for temporary staff. **[Data]**
- **Canopy add-on stack remains:** Even with flat tiers, add-ons persist for Smart Intake ($11/credit), Tax Resolution ($50/user/mo), Transcripts & Notices ($50/user/mo), KBA ($1.25/credit), and payment processing (3.30% + $0.20 per card, 1% cap $10 ACH). **[Data — getcanopy.com/pricing]**

## Pricing Whitespace

Where there's room to position on price — underserved segments, untried models, price points nobody occupies:

- **$29–49/user/mo band** — entirely empty between File In Time amortized (~$8/mo Year 2+) and Karbon Team ($59/mo). DueDateHQ's proposed pricing sits cleanly here. **[Data]**
- **True freemium** — no competitor offers it; market opportunity for top-of-funnel capture. **[Data]**
- **Per-client pricing (not per-seat)** — no competitor uses this. Could work for DueDateHQ Solo tier ("monitor 20 clients for $29/mo") but creates complexity at team scale. **[Opinion — not recommended]**
- **Outcome-based pricing** — "pay only for extensions detected" — would be category-defining but hard to price predictably. **[Opinion — worth exploring for a free-tier conversion ladder]**
- **Under $300/yr solo all-in** — File In Time hits this only after Year 1. Canopy starts at $888/yr, TaxDome $800/yr, Karbon $708/yr. DueDateHQ Pro at $588/yr annual would own this price point. **[Data + Opinion]**

## Recommendations

### If competing on price

Stay disciplined in the $29–49 gap below the cloud-PM floor. **Do not cross $60/user/mo** — crossing that threshold triggers direct feature comparison with Karbon Team ($59) and Canopy Standard ($74), where DueDateHQ loses on platform breadth. **[Opinion]** Maintain $29 Solo and $49 Pro as permanent anchors; let Team tier at $99 stretch toward small-firm use cases.

### If competing on value

Frame the comparison as "one capability nobody else offers" × "price of a streaming service." DueDateHQ at $49/mo ≈ Netflix. Karbon at $708/yr is 12× the monthly commitment. Pivot every ROI conversation to extension-detection: a single missed state extension can cost a CPA $10K+ in penalties + client relationships. $588/yr DueDateHQ pays for itself on one avoided miss. **[Opinion]**

### If competing on model

**Monthly billing by default + 20% annual discount.** Never require annual upfront. This directly attacks TaxDome's biggest friction point and offers the flexibility Karbon-curious solo CPAs want. **[Data — multiple Capterra reviews cite TaxDome annual as friction]**

**Unlimited clients on Pro and Team.** Steal TaxDome's strongest differentiator pattern. DueDateHQ's cost of an additional client is marginal (it's metadata, not storage or portal bandwidth) — pricing should reflect that. **[Data — TaxDome pattern confirmed]**

**Rejected model: per-action AI credit metering.** Canopy uses this for Smart Intake, and reviewer sentiment is clearly negative. Avoid entirely. **[Data — Canopy reviewer quotes]**

**Recommended experimental model: "Guaranteed accuracy" refund.** If DueDateHQ misses a state extension announcement by more than 24 hours, the user's next month is free. Makes the SLA a commercial commitment, not just marketing. Low financial risk if the accuracy SLA holds; high differentiation if it does. **[Opinion]**
